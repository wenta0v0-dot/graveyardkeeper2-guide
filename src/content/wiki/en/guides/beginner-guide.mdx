Graveyard Keeper 2 looks like a burial simulator. It is actually three engines bolted together, and new keepers stall because they feed one while starving the others. This guide explains each engine, the order to unlock them, and where players typically get stuck — every claim checked against **version 1.008** (October 16, 2026).

<Callout type="info" title="The short version">
  Run **three engines** in parallel: the **graveyard and church** (corpses in, faith and money out), the **tech tree and town** (the Royal Foundry line is the long pole — beeline it), and the **automation economy** (zombie workers and factory lines as early as the tech allows). Your day has limited energy; spend it on the engine that is furthest behind.
</Callout>

## Engine 1: The graveyard and the church

This is the loop the game opens with, and it never stops mattering.

- **Graveyard work and burials**: bodies come in, graves go out. **Corpse quality** is a core system — it drives what each burial is worth, so the habits you form around preparing bodies in your first days pay for the whole campaign.
- **Church and sermons**: church work converts your graveyard's output into **faith** and income through sermons. Faith feeds back into more systems, so the earlier the church engine is running, the faster everything else moves.
- **Energy and insanity**: both gate what you can do. Energy limits the work in your day; insanity shapes what the world lets you get away with. New keepers plan too many tasks and not enough rest — the yard rewards a planned week over a heroic day.

## Engine 2: The tech tree and the town

The technology tree is Graveyard Keeper 2's roadmap, and one line on it is longer than all the others.

- **Beeline the Royal Foundry**. Steel and the foundry line are the community's most-stuck progression point — the prerequisite chain is long, and starting it late stalls runs. Read the tech tree on day one and work backwards from the foundry.
- **The town is part of the tech tree**. Town building, orders and trade connect to the same progression web — treat town work as tech-tree work, not as a side trip.
- **Crafting along the way**: crafting and technologies are documented systems in their own right. Craft toward your next unlock, not for its own sake.

## Engine 3: The automation economy

This is where Graveyard Keeper 2 turns from a job into an empire.

- **Farming and fishing** feed the economy early — they are steady inputs while the graveyard engine spins up.
- **The factory and alcohol lines** convert cheap inputs into high-value products. Set them up as soon as the tech allows.
- **Zombie workers** are the finish line: mining, farming and factory stations run by the dead. Every task moved onto a zombie is a task your energy no longer has to pay for. Aim your tech tree at them.

## Three mistakes that stall new keepers

1. **Starting the foundry line late.** Steel blocks more than it seems; the Royal Foundry chain punishes late starts the most. (See [troubleshooting](/guides/troubleshooting/).)
2. **Ignoring corpse quality early.** Rebuilding bodies and habits later costs more than doing it right from the first burial.
3. **Working instead of automating.** If a zombie or a factory line can do a task, your personal energy should not be paying for it.
